The rivals are supplying each other.

A morning edition — rival hardware changing hands, the same proof arriving at two laboratories on one day, and a hearing that starts at two o’clock.

For two years the frontier’s companies have competed for scarce hardware and guarded it closely. This morning’s paper records the guard coming down in two places at once: the processors at the center of the buildout are being sold and hosted across the lines the industry drew around itself, and in a different register the same discovery has arrived at two rival laboratories on the same day, each waiting for outsiders to judge it. Over it all sits today’s calendar — a central bank at two o’clock, then two large earnings reports — which will settle some of this and leave the rest for later.

THE SHORT VERSION

 

01  OpenAI plans to buy the rival’s chips. The Information reports OpenAI will buy Google’s TPUs outright, after renting them since last year. No terms were disclosed, and OpenAI declined to comment.

02  The same refutation, twice. OpenAI and Anthropic say their internal models each disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a conjecture that has stood since 1999 — both results dated to October 16. Outside review comes next.

03  60,000 buyout offers at Microsoft. Announced Monday, the voluntary offer reaches more than a quarter of the workforce — the data centers and security are excluded — two days before tonight’s earnings.

THE LEAD

 
 

From renting to owning.

The reported purchase is the third stage of a widening relationship. Last week OpenAI put its frontier models on Google Cloud; before that it was a tenant for the chips themselves. Owning them outright ends the cloud margin on those hours and puts the hardware on OpenAI’s own books.

It also adds a third supplier to a base that already runs on Nvidia’s processors and a custom chip of OpenAI’s own design, due to deploy by the end of the year. Alphabet’s finance chief has told investors to expect most TPU sales revenue in 2027. What would turn this report into a transaction is still missing: a signature, a volume, a delivery date.
