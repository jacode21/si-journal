Thinking Machines Posted Inkling 2 Small’s Weights for Download, Under the Apache 2.0 Licence

Thinking Machines Lab posted the weights of Inkling 2 Small on Hugging Face on Thursday, November 12, under the Apache 2.0 licence, which allows commercial use. The model launched on November 5 through the lab’s Tinker platform, with the weights held back for a week of outside safety testing. The model stores 594 billion parameters and uses 17 billion for each token it processes, and it reads images as well as text. Its main checkpoint needs a GPU cluster with at least 500GB of combined video memory, the model card says. The lab reports a score of 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 026  ·  Friday, November 13, 2026

The download test has a new result. Thinking Machines Lab introduced Inkling 2 Small on November 5 and kept it on its own Tinker platform while outside safety testing and red-teaming — adversarial attempts to expose problems — were completed. On Thursday it posted the full weights on Hugging Face, the standard public repository, under the Apache 2.0 licence. Anyone may download the model, run it on their own machines, fine-tune it and build commercial products on it.

The name needs translating. Inkling 2 Small stores 594 billion parameters — the learned values inside the model — and activates 17 billion of them for each token it processes, routing each token to 8 of 384 specialist sub-networks. It accepts images as well as text. The lab reports a score of 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, against the first Inkling generation’s far lower marks; the model card puts it at 30.6. Those figures are the lab’s.

Downloadable is not the same as small. The main checkpoint, in a compressed format called NVFP4, needs a cluster with at least 500GB of combined graphics memory — the model card gives two NVIDIA B300 chips, or four H200s, as examples. A second checkpoint needs about 800GB. For teams without a cluster, the model is also served by API providers including NVIDIA, Fireworks, Together, Baseten, Nebius, Modal and Cloudflare; Together lists $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.00 per million output tokens.

The paper set this test in Edition 017 and applied it across the robot models in Edition 025: a model is open when its weights can be downloaded, not when a company says the word. Inkling 2 Small was announced as an open model and, a week late by its own schedule, now passes in full — files, licence and all.
