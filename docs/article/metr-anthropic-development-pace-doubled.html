Outside Auditors Find Anthropic Is Improving Its Models About Twice as Fast as a Year Ago, Mostly by Using AI to Help

METR, the nonprofit evaluator, spent 11 weeks with Anthropic's internal data and concluded its internal capability index has grown 1.9 times faster since October 2025 than before — with AI assistance, not added compute, as the leading explanation. Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 019  ·  Friday, November 6, 2026

METR, the Berkeley nonprofit that evaluates frontier models, published an independent assessment on Thursday after spending 11 weeks, from mid-August to late October, working with Anthropic's internal data: pretraining, reinforcement learning, compute figures, internal benchmarks and speed-up analyses, plus about 15 staff interviews. Its central finding is that Anthropic's internal capability index — a composite of benchmark scores — has grown at 1.9 times its earlier rate since October 2025, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5 to 2.8 times. In plain terms, METR says Anthropic made roughly two years of its historical progress in the last year: a distance it compares to the gap between Claude 2.1 and Sonnet 4.5. The count includes three models Anthropic has finished since July and not released.

METR says it can confidently rule out faster growth in training compute as the explanation. Its leading hypothesis — stated as a guess, at perhaps 80% of the acceleration — is that AI is doing more of the research: writing code, running analyses, and assisting the training of the next models. The group is careful about the boundary of the claim. The measured acceleration is on well-specified tasks, the kind that can be graded automatically; it may not extend to messier work. And Anthropic exercised a right to redact non-public information: METR says the company redacted a majority of the evidence the auditors found most informative, which Anthropic describes as removing material that could accelerate other developers. Readers are asked to take the headline conclusion substantially on METR's word.

A companion METR review of Anthropic's August risk report adds a sharper edge: it judges that the public report understates the acceleration that was occurring, and that it is plausible the company has crossed the doubling threshold its own framework treats as an early warning — a judgment Anthropic's report does not accept. The assessment lands as Anthropic prepares its roadshow in the week of November 9, and weeks after chief executive Dario Amodei published an essay urging the industry to pace the frontier so safety work can keep up. Both facts belong in the same file: the lab asking for a slower frontier is, by this audit, moving about twice as fast as it was.
