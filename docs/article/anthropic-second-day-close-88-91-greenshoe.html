Anthropic Closed Its Second Day at $88.91, Down 8.5%, After Trading Five Cents Above Its $83 IPO Price

Anthropic shares opened their second day at $89.86, fell to a low of $83.05 — five cents above the $83 offering price — and closed at $88.91, down 8.50% from Thursday’s $97.17 close (Finnhub; Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance data via market coverage, Nov 20). The offering itself grew: underwriters fully exercised their option to buy 147 million additional shares, lifting the total sold to about 1.127 billion shares and gross proceeds to about $93.5 billion, the company said. At Friday’s close Anthropic was worth about $1.16 trillion on shares outstanding, by Dow Jones Market Data.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 034  ·  Saturday, November 21, 2026

The second day tested the offering price and held it, barely. Anthropic opened at $89.86, down 7.5% from Thursday’s close, and traded as low as $83.05 in the session — five cents above the price at which 980 million shares were sold on Wednesday evening (Finnhub quote snapshot, Nov 20; MarketWatch). The close was $88.91, down $8.26, or 8.50%. After hours the shares were quoted at $87.80. Friday’s fall gave back more than half of Thursday’s 17% debut gain, in a session when the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% and chip stocks fell further; the move was a sector move as well as a company move, and the sources do not separate the two.

The deal underneath the price got bigger on Friday. Underwriters fully exercised the greenshoe — their option to buy additional shares at the offering price — adding 147 million shares to the 980 million sold, for about 1.127 billion shares in total and gross proceeds of about $93.5 billion including the option shares (company statement via market coverage, Nov 20). Alongside the public offering, the concurrent $26 billion private placement sold 313,253,007 Class N shares at $83 to seven companies: AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC (company disclosures, reported Nov 20). Retail investors received about 15% of the offering, against a planned 20% and a typical 5% to 10% (CNBC, reported Nov 20).

What happens next is mechanical before it is judgmental. The offering was expected to close on Friday. The stock joins the Morningstar US Total Market Index after five trading days, which falls on Wednesday, November 25. The first full week of trading will show whether the $83 line — the price the underwriters sold, and the low the market touched on day two — is a floor set by the deal or only the first level the fall happened to pause at.
