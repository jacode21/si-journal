The Federal Reserve Held Rates at 3.75% to 4%, With Two Officials Voting to Raise Them

The 10–2 decision kept the target range set by September’s increase. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack preferred a further quarter-point rise; the statement called growth strong and inflation elevated.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 011  ·  Thursday, October 29, 2026

The Federal Open Market Committee voted 10–2 on Wednesday to hold the federal funds target range at 3.75% to 4%, the range set by September’s quarter-point increase — the Fed’s first increase since 2023. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voted against the decision, preferring to raise the range by a further quarter point. Futures markets had put the probability of a hold at about 90% before the meeting (CME FedWatch).

The statement upgraded its description of growth: “Economic activity is expanding at a strong pace,” up from “solid.” It described domestic spending as resilient, productivity growth as strong and capital investment as robust, said job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and said inflation “remains elevated” — consumer prices rose 3.7% year over year in September, up from 3.4% in August, against a 2% goal. At his press conference, Chair Kevin Warsh described “a fairly heated debate internally, with serious and careful exchanges,” and said inflation was still “too high.” The statement gave no commitment about the next move. The next meeting is December 8–9; market reports put futures odds of a December increase near 85%.

For the AI buildout, the decision holds the financing weather in place rather than changing it. The borrowing costs behind the lease vehicles, forward sales and guarantees documented in Editions 007 to 009 are set against a policy rate that two voting members already want higher, and a 10-year Treasury yield that closed Wednesday at about 5.05%. The next evidence arrives this morning: September PCE inflation, the Fed’s preferred measure, and the first estimate of third-quarter GDP, both at 8:30 a.m.
