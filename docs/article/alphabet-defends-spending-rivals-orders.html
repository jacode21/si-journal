Tonight, Alphabet Defends Its Spending With Orders From Its Rivals

Wednesday’s hearing was set up as a test of whether cloud growth justifies the capital spending. Tuesday’s chip stories changed what Alphabet can put in evidence: its largest rival plans to buy its processors, and a second cloud will rent them out.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 010  ·  Wednesday, October 28, 2026

The hearing this paper has tracked for a week opens at 2 p.m., when the Federal Reserve decides — futures on this paper’s record imply roughly a 90% chance of a hold at 3.75–4.00% — and continues after the close, when Microsoft and Alphabet report. The questions were set in advance. For Microsoft: Azure growth guided at about 45% in constant currency, against quarterly capital spending guided above $50 billion. For Alphabet: Google Cloud growth against a 2026 capex guide of $195 to $205 billion. Edition 009’s Long View noted that the hearing grades only the listed half of the buildout. What changed overnight is the evidence available to one of the defendants.

On Tuesday, The Information reported that OpenAI plans to buy Google’s TPUs outright, and Oracle announced it will host Google’s eighth-generation TPUs from 2027 — the first time the chips run on a rival cloud. Anthropic’s demand for TPUs was already part of Alphabet’s story; its prospectus itemizes more than $160 billion of Google chips taken through Broadcom. Add a reported outright purchase by the largest independent lab and a hosting deal with a fourth hyperscaler, and the capex question changes shape. It is no longer only whether Google Cloud’s growth covers the spending. It is whether Alphabet is building the industry’s silicon supply — capacity its competitors must buy from it — in which case the spending is partly a merchant business, with the revenue recognized mostly in 2027, as its finance chief has already told investors to expect.

Microsoft arrives with the opposite kind of evidence. Its numbers tonight will be read beside Monday’s memo: buyouts offered to about 60,000 employees, with the data centers and the security organization outside the offer. That is cost discipline aimed at everything except the buildout — the clearest statement yet, from the largest spender, of which payroll the AI program is allowed to touch.

What tonight still cannot settle is the larger part of this week’s record. The TPU purchase has no terms, no volume and no signature. Oracle’s TPUs arrive in 2027. The financed buildout — the lease vehicles, the guarantees, the forward sales documented in Editions 007 to 009 — reports on contract clocks, not quarterly ones, and its next date is Friday’s bridge signatures. Tonight grades the listed layer with better evidence than expected. The other layer keeps its own calendar.
