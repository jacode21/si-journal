Apple Reports Record September-Quarter Revenue of $114.5 Billion and Guides Holiday-Quarter Margins Lower

Apple's fiscal fourth quarter beat expectations on revenue and earnings: iPhone revenue set a September-quarter record and Services revenue reached an all-time high. The December-quarter guide carries the pressure — revenue growth of 7% to 10% with gross margin of 45% to 46%, down from 47.9%, as memory costs rise. Shares fell after the report.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 016  ·  Tuesday, November 3, 2026

Apple reported fiscal fourth-quarter results on Monday, November 2, for the quarter ended September 26: revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12% from a year earlier, and diluted earnings per share of $2.10, up 14%. Both figures beat the expectations carried into the report — about $113.35 billion of revenue and $1.99 per share in the Yahoo Finance estimates cited in coverage of the results. Net income was $30.71 billion, and the quarter closed a fiscal year with record revenue of $479 billion. It was Apple's first report under chief executive John Ternus.

The product lines split. iPhone revenue reached $57.58 billion, up 17% and a September-quarter record. Services revenue reached an all-time high of $31.57 billion, and the company said paid subscriptions passed 1.5 billion. Mac revenue rose 24% to $10.79 billion, which the earnings-call summary attributed to demand for the MacBook Neo and MacBook Pro. iPad revenue fell 10% to $6.2 billion and Wearables, Home and Accessories fell 8% to $8.3 billion, both against strong launches a year earlier. Gross margin was 47.9%, including about one percentage point from tariff refunds worth $0.05 of earnings per share.

The guide is where the report tightened. For the December quarter — Apple's largest of the year — the company expects revenue growth of 7% to 10%, which would still make it the largest revenue quarter in its history, and gross margin of 45% to 46%, down from 47.9%, citing higher memory costs. One widely circulated analysis of the report put Greater China revenue at $17.54 billion against about $18.75 billion modeled, the largest single miss in its reading. Apple shares had closed Monday down 1.83% at $338.32 before the report and fell roughly a further 3% in after-hours trading.
