CoreWeave Beat Its Own Revenue Forecast, and Its Interest Bill Passed $846 Million in One Quarter

CoreWeave reported third-quarter results after Tuesday’s close. Revenue was $3.661 billion, up 168% from a year earlier and above the top of its own $3.45 billion to $3.60 billion guidance. Adjusted operating income was $379 million, against guidance of $200 million to $260 million. Net interest expense was $846 million, up from $310 million a year earlier, and the net loss was $709 million. Revenue backlog reached about $132.5 billion, and the company raised its full-year revenue guidance to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 024  ·  Wednesday, November 11, 2026

The quarter CoreWeave promised in this paper’s agenda arrived with both halves of the neocloud argument in the same filing. The operating half beat everything: revenue of $3.661 billion, up 168% year over year and 42% on the second quarter — the first quarter in which revenue grew by more than $1 billion — and adjusted operating income of $379 million, well above the $200 million to $260 million the company had guided. Adjusted EBITDA was $2.256 billion, a 62% margin. Backlog — contracted amounts not yet recognised as revenue, subject to delivery — reached about $132.5 billion, from $104.2 billion at the end of June. Active power doubled since the end of 2025 to 1.7 gigawatts.

The financing half is the same size as the operating half, and growing faster. Net interest expense was $846 million for the quarter, up from $310 million a year earlier and $640 million in the second quarter; the company guided to $1.05 billion to $1.15 billion for the fourth quarter. Against $207 million of GAAP operating income, the interest bill produced a net loss of $709 million. Total debt stood at $46.873 billion at September 30. Capital expenditure guidance for 2026 rose to $37 billion to $39 billion. Management raised the full-year revenue guide to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion and the exit run-rate target to $20 billion to $21 billion, and said the 2028 capacity it has brought to market is already sold out. The filing does not resolve the argument; it prices it. CoreWeave’s operations now earn real money at scale, and its lenders earn roughly four times as much from the same quarter.
