The Information Reports DeepSeek and Moonshot Trained Models on OpenAI’s Output

The report, carried by Reuters on Wednesday, says outputs from OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra were used heavily in training Moonshot’s Kimi K3.1 Pro and DeepSeek’s upgraded V4.1-Flash. None of the three companies commented on the report.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 011  ·  Thursday, October 29, 2026

The Information reported on Wednesday that DeepSeek and Moonshot AI relied heavily on outputs from OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra to train their latest models, citing people with direct knowledge of the matter; Reuters carried the report the same day. The models named are Moonshot’s Kimi K3.1 Pro, released last week, and DeepSeek’s upgraded V4.1-Flash 1017, launched October 17. OpenAI, DeepSeek and Moonshot did not immediately respond to Reuters’ requests for comment, and the report is written that way here: an attributed claim, not an established fact.

Distillation means training a smaller or newer model on text a stronger model produced, so the student reproduces part of the teacher’s capability at a fraction of the training cost. OpenAI said in a September 30 blog post that it had linked part of a distillation campaign that began in July to individuals associated with Moonshot. Anthropic has said new measures against the practice have reduced data harvested from Claude in such campaigns by about 90% to 99%. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google have each argued the practice lets rivals reproduce advanced capability more quickly and cheaply than building it directly.

Kimi K3.1 Pro’s capability is not in question in this paper’s record — its company-reported benchmarks were covered in Edition 004, with their sourcing stated. What the report changes is the account of where part of that capability came from, days before Moonshot’s promised release of the model’s full weights by November 4. If the weights ship on schedule, outside researchers will be able to test the model directly; the training-data question will remain a matter of the companies’ records, not the artifact.
