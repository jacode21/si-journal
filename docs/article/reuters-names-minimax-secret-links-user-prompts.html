Reuters Named MiniMax as the Chinese AI Firm Whose Systems Opened Secret Links Hidden in User Prompts

Reuters reported on Thursday, November 26, that confidential data submitted to the Chinese AI company MiniMax was accessed by unidentified parties, according to HD Moore, chief research officer at the US cybersecurity firm Dragos. Dragos had described the pattern in a November 19 investigation without naming the company: private web links planted in requests sent to AI services were later opened by parties who should never have seen them. Moore told Reuters one secret link sent to MiniMax later appeared in a chat channel on Feishu, ByteDance’s workplace platform, and was opened by devices in Beijing and Shanghai, 24 days after it was submitted.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 039  ·  Thursday, November 26, 2026

MiniMax answered on Tuesday, before Reuters named it. The company said its security system had collected and stored web links contained in messages sent to its models through other providers, and automatically opened some of them to check for security threats. It said it did not retain full conversations, had deleted the stored addresses, and had stopped the activity; it said it found no evidence, in the requests it examined, that conversations or responses were disclosed to third parties. Atlas Cloud, a US provider that offered access to MiniMax’s models, said that explanation matched its own findings, stopped forwarding requests to MiniMax, and warned customers who used its MiniMax models to treat any secrets they submitted as potentially exposed.

The wider investigation matters as much as the name. Dragos sent close to two million test prompts carrying unique fake passwords and tracking links through AI routing services between July and November. OpenRouter, the largest of those routers, said it was not itself breached; it removed three providers and pulled 125 endpoints from zero-retention status on November 22. Chinese models were the three most used on OpenRouter in October, Reuters reports, measured by text processed — which is why a link-handling practice at one provider reaches customers who may never have chosen it directly. The distinction to keep: MiniMax says its own security system opened the links; Dragos’s evidence shows the links were accessed from places and at times the senders did not intend. Both accounts agree the links were opened.
