Immersion Cooling’s 492-Day Problem

The synthetic oil cooling AI data centres quietly swells the rubber seals of ordinary capacitors; an accelerated-ageing model puts structural failure at about 492 days at 50 °C.
Issue No. 007  ·  Monday, October 26, 2026

The synthetic oil cooling AI data centres quietly swells the rubber seals of ordinary capacitors; an accelerated-ageing model puts structural failure at about 492 days at 50 °C.

What it is

Researchers at the University of Science and Technology Beijing, with ZTE, aged surface-mount aluminium electrolytic capacitors — ubiquitous in data-centre hardware — in synthetic immersion-cooling oil. The failure mode is not electrical drift: the coolant infiltrates the components’ butyl rubber seals and swells them until the part physically deforms and cannot be seated. A second path: the conductive polymer PEDOT de-dopes, raising resistance. Accelerated ageing at 70–100 °C, extrapolated through an Arrhenius model, predicts structural failure in about 492 days at 50 °C.

Why it matters

Immersion cooling is a core bet for high-density AI data centres — and on this evidence, a cheap rubber seal, not the chips, may set the maintenance clock.

What is not yet known

A laboratory accelerated-ageing study: the 492-day figure is a model extrapolation to 50 °C, not a field observation, and one synthetic oil formulation was tested — other coolants may behave differently.

Evidence — Early research

Paper in npj Materials Degradation, published Oct 26, 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41529-026-00898-2); funded by ZTE, disclosed in the paper.
