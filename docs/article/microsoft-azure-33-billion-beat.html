Microsoft Beat Estimates as Azure Revenue Rose 48% to $33.2 Billion

Microsoft disclosed Azure’s revenue in dollars for the first time — $33.17 billion, above the $32.32 billion analysts expected — as total revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion, capital spending came in below estimates, and the shares rose 3.76% in extended trading.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 011  ·  Thursday, October 29, 2026

Microsoft reported fiscal first-quarter results after Wednesday’s close. Revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion, above the $90.69 billion LSEG consensus. Adjusted earnings were $4.89 a share, against $4.73 expected. Operating income rose 21% to $45.9 billion, and net income rose 32% to $36.6 billion; the net figure includes a $207 million gain from Microsoft’s investments in OpenAI, where the year-earlier quarter recorded a $3.1 billion loss on the same line. Excluding the investment effects in both quarters, net income rose 18% to $36.4 billion.

The report carried a disclosure Microsoft has never made before: Azure’s revenue in dollars. Azure brought in $33.17 billion in the quarter, up 48% year over year, up from 42% growth in the June quarter and above the $32.32 billion analysts expected. The figure arrives with a new reporting structure that splits the company into two segments — Agents and Infra, with revenue up 25% to $76.8 billion, and Devices and Consumer. Microsoft Cloud revenue was $63.9 billion, up 30%, and commercial remaining performance obligation — contracted revenue not yet recognized — rose 73% to $677 billion. Chief Executive Satya Nadella told analysts Microsoft 365 Copilot had 50% more users than in the June quarter; the company did not disclose paid Copilot seats. The consumer side was weaker: Windows OEM and devices revenue fell 22%, and Xbox content and services revenue fell 2%.

On the spending side, capital expenditures and finance leases together were $45.2 billion, up 30% year over year but below the $50.67 billion analysts expected. Free cash flow was $18.63 billion — positive, and down 27% from a year earlier. Shares rose 3.76% in extended trading to $550.95, after closing the regular session at $531.01 (CNBC market data). Monday’s voluntary buyout offer to about 60,000 employees (Edition 010) is the cost side of the same account: payroll offered for reduction everywhere except the data centers, security and sales, in the quarter the buildout’s returns were measured.
