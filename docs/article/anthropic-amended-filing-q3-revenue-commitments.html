Anthropic's Amended IPO Filing Puts Third-Quarter Revenue at $17 Billion and Commitments at $895 Billion

Anthropic filed the first amendment to its IPO prospectus on Monday, adding a third quarter the original filing did not cover. Revenue reached $17.0 billion, up 47% from the second quarter, and the company swung back to an operating loss after paying $5.0 billion for short-term compute. Its commitments for compute and infrastructure now total about $895.4 billion. The amendment still carries no share count, no price range and no listing date.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 016  ·  Tuesday, November 3, 2026

Anthropic filed Amendment No. 1 to its IPO registration statement on Monday, November 2 (SEC Form S-1/A), adding the third quarter to a prospectus that originally covered only the first half of the year. Revenue for the three months ended September 30 was $16.956 billion — about $17.0 billion — up about 47% from the second quarter's $11.555 billion and roughly twelve times the same quarter a year earlier. Revenue for the nine months reached $33.242 billion. The growth rate slowed: the second quarter had grown about 144% over the first. In absolute terms the company still added $5.4 billion of revenue in one quarter, less than the $6.8 billion it added in the second.

The quarter's cost

Compute and infrastructure cost $9.863 billion in the third quarter, 58% of revenue, up from 48% in the second quarter. The company swung to a $567 million operating loss, after $974 million of operating income in the second quarter, and attributed the loss primarily to $5.0 billion of compute bought under short-term commitments. The headline net loss was far larger and mostly not cash: $23.29 billion for the quarter, including $21.9 billion of noncash fair-value losses on convertible notes and related instruments, and $104 billion for the nine months, including $94.3 billion of such changes, CNBC reported from the filing. Cash and short-term investments stood at about $131.8 billion at September 30.

The commitments

The amendment puts the company's aggregate purchase commitments and leases at about $895.4 billion, substantially all of it for compute and infrastructure, running through 2045, with about half expected by the end of 2030. The filing also discloses developer guarantees of up to $4.7 billion. The $895.4 billion supersedes the $542–567 billion binding count this paper quoted from the original filing in Edition 008: the amendment's schedule is broader and is now the figure on record. Set against the third quarter, the commitments total roughly thirteen years of revenue at the current quarterly rate — a comparison that will move quickly if revenue keeps compounding, and that states the scale of what has been promised before the shares are priced.

A new risk, stated in the filing

The amendment also adds risk language about the company's own research process, reported by TechCrunch from the filing text. Anthropic states that its increasing use of AI systems to accelerate its own research and development may cause its models' capabilities to advance more rapidly than its ability to develop and implement safety, alignment and security measures, and that internal estimates for future models — not current ones — indicate it may imminently meet its Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of 'dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI.' No current model has been designated as having crossed that threshold. The filing adds that the company does not anticipate being able to safely use Claude to fully autonomously design and build successive generations of frontier models without meaningful human supervision, and that it is considering updates to the policy to keep humans in control of frontier model development.

What the amendment does not contain is the offering itself: no share count, no price range and no deal size, so this is not the amendment that starts the roadshow. The roadshow remains reported for the week of November 9, with a mid-November listing discussed. Edition 013 recorded OpenAI's bridge round closing at a reduced size against a $1.25 trillion valuation negotiated in private, without a public Anthropic price to argue against. That comparison now has a date.

The Bigger Picture

Anthropic is now the reference point for the frontier, whether or not its own shares have priced. OpenAI's bridge closed in October at a valuation negotiated in private, and the hyperscalers' spending plans were set against private revenue claims. A public roadshow converts one laboratory's revenue, costs and commitments into a market price that the others will be measured against — including a commitment schedule of $895 billion that no private round had to defend in public.
