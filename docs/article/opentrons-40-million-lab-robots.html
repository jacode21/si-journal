Opentrons Raised $40 Million to Put AI-Driven Laboratory Robots in More Labs

Opentrons builds open, programmable robots that run laboratory experiments, and says more than 11,000 of them are deployed in over 80 countries. The $40 million Series E, led by Avego, funds the software and data layer that lets AI systems design, run and revise experiments in a continuous loop.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 012  ·  Friday, October 30, 2026

Opentrons Labworks announced a $40 million Series E financing on Thursday, led by the healthcare investor Avego with participation from BroadOak Capital Partners. Existing investors include SoftBank Vision Fund and Khosla Ventures. The company, founded in 2014 and based in Long Island City, New York, is led by chief executive James Atwood. It did not disclose a valuation.

Opentrons builds laboratory robots — the Flex platform — that are open and programmable through an API, so experiments can be specified in software, run by the robot, and repeated identically in another laboratory. The company says more than 11,000 of its robots are deployed in laboratories in over 80 countries, including at 90% of the top 20 global pharmaceutical companies and every top-15 US research university. Those deployment figures are the company's. The new capital goes to scaling its robotics, software and data infrastructure, to AI-driven experimental execution, and to expansion into new markets.

The company's stated problem is a sequencing gap in AI-driven science: frontier models can now generate hypotheses and design experiments faster than physical laboratories can run them, so the bottleneck moves from the model to the bench. Opentrons' answer is to make the bench programmable — each experiment run on its robots produces structured, reproducible data that the next round of model work can use. The round lands in a competitive stretch for laboratory automation: the British company Automata raised $45 million in February, and Multiply Labs raised $75 million this month for robotic biomanufacturing (Dealroom). What distinguishes the claims here is installed base: Opentrons is selling an execution layer on top of 11,000 machines already in laboratories, not asking laboratories to adopt a first machine.
