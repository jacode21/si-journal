Meta Plans to Raise Up to $95 Billion in Stock, Its Largest Offering Ever

About $35 billion of offerings launched Monday, with an at-the-market program of up to $60 billion to follow — and Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom reported interested in buying up to $5 billion of it.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 008  ·  Tuesday, October 27, 2026

Meta Platforms said Monday it plans to raise up to $95 billion in its largest-ever equity fundraising (Reuters). About $35 billion of public offerings — common and preferred stock — launched the same day; a separate at-the-market program of up to $60 billion is planned once Meta’s new non-voting Class C shares begin trading, expected in the first quarter of 2027, with timing and terms not yet final.

The detail that drew the market’s attention: Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom — the chip suppliers Meta’s spending enriches — have expressed interest in buying up to $5 billion combined of the Class A shares. Reuters notes the circularity plainly: suppliers helping to finance the expansion of one of their biggest customers. William Blair’s analysts, in a note: “We expect investors to continue to question the financing and potential for future capital raises,” while crediting the move as evidence of Meta’s confidence in the returns.

The context is this paper’s credit thread. Meta has committed roughly $825 billion to future spending, most of it data centres, AI infrastructure and cloud capacity (Reuters). Its third-quarter free cash flow was −$2.12 billion — the first negative print since 2012 (Edition 003) — and its five-year credit protection set a record at 114.79 basis points on October 22 (Edition 005). Shares fell 1.4% in the morning and closed down 1.88%. Alphabet increased the size of its own equity offerings to $84.75 billion earlier this year (Reuters): for the hyperscalers, the marginal dollar for the buildout is now coming from shareholders, not just lenders.

Status, labeled: the $35 billion of offerings are launched, not priced in the reporting; the $60 billion program is a plan whose start depends on the Class C listing. What is established is the direction — the largest spender in the buildout has chosen dilution over additional debt, in a week when Wednesday’s hearing will grade the spending itself.
