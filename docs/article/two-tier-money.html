Two-Tier Money

Protection on CoreWeave ended last week above 1,100 basis points — a coin-flip default price. Protection on Nvidia trades at records too. The buildout now has two prices of money, and Sunday’s post drew the line between them.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 007  ·  Monday, October 26, 2026

The credit ladder, as Morgan Stanley’s prints have it: CoreWeave’s five-year CDS ended last week above 1,100 basis points (MarketWatch, this morning) — a level the market reads as implying roughly a 50% probability of default within five years. The ladder below and above it: Oracle at 322, SoftBank at 424, SpaceX at a record 226 — and, at the top of the stack, records of a different kind: Meta at 108 and Nvidia at 101, names whose protection was an afterthought a year ago, when the hyperscaler basket averaged under 40 basis points. Weekly CDS volume on the big AI names averaged over $9 billion in September, against under $2 billion in July (Morgan Stanley via Bloomberg).

Sunday made the ladder a narrative. Elon Musk posted that SpaceX had closed $40 billion of GPU debt because it is investment grade, with revenue independent of AI data centers — and that the 200-plus neoclouds face “guppy-level death and consolidation” (see Latest). CoreWeave and Nebius fell more than 2% premarket; Morgan Stanley’s Mike Wilson, the same morning, called semiconductors and the capex beneficiaries the “epicenter” of funding concerns and put the SOX’s support 10–15% below Friday’s close.

The long view is the structure, not the spread. The buildout is financed: $466 billion of AI-linked debt issued so far in 2026, against $101 billion in the same period of 2025 (Morgan Stanley) — with wider perimeters putting the sector’s 2026 borrowing near $850 billion (Finance Chief; this paper, Edition 006 — different measures of the same flood). At the center, Nvidia has preliminary agreements with six of the largest private-capital firms targeting more than $500 billion in third-party capital, with an option to backstop $125 billion of it: the chipmaker as arranger of the money that buys the chips.

Wednesday gives the structure its hearing: the Fed at 2 p.m., Microsoft and Alphabet after the close, Amazon on Thursday. Two-tier money is not a crisis; it is a price. What Wednesday decides is what the next year of the buildout gets charged.
