Priced Off a Leak

Anthropic is being valued off a draft no investor can audit, and OpenAI’s $1.25 trillion is being priced off Anthropic. Neither number is anchored to a document the public can read. Friday and the filing — whichever comes first — are the anchors.
Issue No. 007  ·  Monday, October 26, 2026

Two of the largest private valuations in history are being set this week, and neither is being set by a document. Anthropic’s price is being discovered from a leaked draft — revenue, commitments and governance quoted by reporters, unaudited and unconfirmed. OpenAI’s bridge, due to collect signatures by Friday at $1.25 trillion, is reportedly being held by some investors until Anthropic is priced first (FXStreet, this morning — single-source, and printed as such in Latest). Each deal is the other’s comparable. There is no third anchor.

This is how late-stage frontier pricing now works: a ladder of reported numbers, each rung a negotiation. OpenAI’s ask fell $150 billion in a month (Editions 003–005). Anthropic’s talk fell from $2 trillion toward $1.5 trillion on a single quarter’s deceleration — a quarter in which revenue still rose 45%. The figures may all be accurate. Accuracy is not the problem. The problem is that a draft can be revised in ways a filing cannot, and everyone pricing off it knows it.

The credit market, characteristically, has stopped waiting for documents: protection on CoreWeave ended last week above 1,100 basis points, and protection on Nvidia itself trades at records (see The Long View). Credit is pricing the buildout from cash flows and covenants — things that exist whether or not an S-1 does.

The verdict: this paper has followed the IPO clock since Edition 001, and the clock still matters — a filing converts this week’s reported numbers into audited ones and starts the fifteen-day countdown in fact instead of in talk. Until it lands, every frontier valuation in print should carry the label the draft carries in this edition: reported, not filed.

Update — Monday, 9:05 a.m.: the filing has landed (see The Lead). The draft reporting matched the document on every load-bearing figure — revenue, the paper loss, the commitments, the governance. The Position’s narrower claim was never that the leak was wrong; it was that a price set on a leak is a price set on nothing enforceable. As of 9 a.m., the anchor exists. What the market does with a sworn version of numbers it had already traded on is the week’s real test — starting with Friday’s bridge signatures.

Show the reasoning

FXStreet (Oct 26) reports some prospective OpenAI bridge investors want Anthropic priced first — single-source, flagged in the story.

OpenAI’s ask fell $150 billion from the $1.4 trillion pitch within a month (Editions 003–005); Anthropic’s talk slipped from ~$2 trillion toward ~$1.5 trillion on the Q3 deceleration (Reuters, Oct 15).

Counterpoint: draft figures reported by Reuters and the Wall Street Journal have repeatedly matched later filings in this cycle — “reported, not filed” is a status, not an accusation of error.

The Position’s claim is about anchors, not accuracy: neither valuation is yet tied to a public, audited document.
