Model ML Raised $250 Million at a $1.5 Billion Valuation to Give Every Banker and Consultant a Personal Agent

Model ML, founded in London in 2024 by brothers Chaz and Arnie Englander, announced a $250 million Series B on Tuesday, November 17, co-led by General Atlantic and QED Investors, at a $1.5 billion valuation. The company sells personal AI agents to banks, consultancies and asset managers: an agent that builds financial models, drafts pitch decks and writes due-diligence reports inside the firm’s own systems, working in Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook. The company says annual recurring revenue grew twelvefold year over year in the third quarter and daily active users twenty-sixfold; it discloses no absolute figures for either. Bloomberg reported in September that the company was seeking $100 million to $150 million; the round closed at $250 million.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 032  ·  Thursday, November 19, 2026

The design choice that separates Model ML from the labs is where the work runs. The agents deploy inside a customer’s own infrastructure, so client data does not leave the firm’s environment, and the company says customer data is never used to train models. It is certified to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and audited under SOC 2 Type II. The platform is not tied to one model provider: it benchmarks models against real financial work and routes each task by quality, speed and cost, and a growing share runs on the company’s own fine-tuned open models, which it says cost about 2% of frontier models on the tasks it targets. That cost claim is the company’s, as are the growth multiples.

The customer list is the checkable part. Trade reporting on the round names PwC, which began a global rollout of agents built with Model ML in July 2026, and Deloitte, which signed a multi-year agreement covering document review and due diligence, alongside HSBC, Santander and BDO India. The Englanders are repeat founders: their two previous companies, Fat Llama and Fancy, were both Y Combinator-backed and sold. Total funding is now $350 million, eighteen months after the company left stealth. What the round prices is a position between the labs and the professions: the models are rented, the workflow and the trust are owned.
