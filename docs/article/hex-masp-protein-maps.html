‘Satellite Maps’ of Thousands of Proteins Across Whole Tissue

A University at Buffalo method, hex-MASP, maps where thousands of proteins — and antibody drugs — actually go inside tissue, and has already flagged candidate Alzheimer’s targets.
Issue No. 006  ·  Sunday, October 25, 2026

A University at Buffalo method, hex-MASP, maps where thousands of proteins — and antibody drugs — actually go inside tissue, and has already flagged candidate Alzheimer’s targets.

What it is

Researchers at the University at Buffalo (Jun Qu’s lab) published hex-MASP in PNAS: a method that maps the spatial distribution of thousands of proteins across entire tissue samples, and tracks where monoclonal antibody drugs actually go inside the brain.

Why it matters

The team has already used it to identify candidate Alzheimer’s drug targets and to check whether antibody candidates reach their intended targets before costly clinical trials.

Evidence — Early research

Paper: Huo et al., PNAS 2026 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2532946123), reported by MedicalXpress on Oct 24, 2026.
