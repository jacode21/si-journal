OpenAI Cut Its Funding Target From $30 Billion to $20 Billion at a $1.25 Trillion Valuation

OpenAI cut the target for its bridge financing by a third on the day investor commitments were due, the Financial Times reported on Friday. The $1.25 trillion valuation stayed where the October talks had put it. The first close is expected to raise less than the new $20 billion target, and the Wall Street Journal reported that less than half of the original $30 billion had been raised.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 013  ·  Saturday, October 31, 2026

OpenAI has cut the target for its new funding round from up to $30 billion to about $20 billion, the Financial Times reported on Friday, in a report carried by Reuters. The valuation is unchanged in this step at $1.25 trillion before new money. OpenAI set Friday, October 30, as the deadline for investors to commit. According to the Reuters account of the FT report, some investors reduced their allocations, and the first close of the round is expected to raise less than $20 billion. The account does not name the investors who cut back. OpenAI did not immediately respond to Reuters' request for comment. Later on Friday, the Wall Street Journal reported that OpenAI has raised less than half of its original $30 billion target in the round.

Who is in the smaller round

The FT reported that a consortium of investors from the United Arab Emirates, including the state-backed fund MGX, is expected to be the largest participant, and that under the arrangement OpenAI would gain an option to lease newly developed computing capacity in the region. Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz are also expected to participate, according to the FT report. Wealthy individuals are expected to be offered a subsequent retail offering at the same $1.25 trillion valuation. Those participation details are reported, not announced; OpenAI has made no statement about the round.

The round's path, on this paper's record

The financing has been repriced twice in five weeks. OpenAI first pitched a $1.4 trillion valuation (Edition 003). On October 22, CNBC and Bloomberg reported the valuation in talks at $1.25 trillion with up to $30 billion still sought, led by Coatue and UAE investors (Edition 003–004). Signatures were then targeted for Friday, October 30 (Editions 004–012), with some backers wanting Anthropic priced first (Edition 007) — a sequence that could not happen, since Anthropic's roadshow is scheduled for the week of November 9 (Edition 008). Friday's report resolves the collision by reducing the amount instead of waiting for the rival's price. OpenAI's last completed round, closed March 31, 2026, raised $122 billion in committed capital at an $852 billion post-money valuation, anchored by Amazon, Nvidia and SoftBank, according to the published accounts carried in Friday's coverage. The new round is described in that coverage as interim funding — a bridge toward a planned listing in the first half of 2027.

The cut landed on the week's hardest day for the sector's public prices. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% on Friday (see Latest), and after the close S&P Global Ratings turned its outlook on Meta negative, citing uncertainty over how long Meta's heavy capital spending will last, according to a summary of the action. Anthropic's roadshow, in the week of November 9, will now produce the first price for a frontier lab that a public market has set. OpenAI's $1.25 trillion remains a negotiated figure: agreed for the smaller amount investors funded on Friday, and untested at the $30 billion size at which it was first offered.
