A Tiny Robot Arm Powered by Living Algae and Light

TU Delft’s two-centimetre arm bends when light hits the algae embedded in it — no motor, no wires — and has grasped and lifted small objects.
Issue No. 006  ·  Sunday, October 25, 2026

TU Delft’s two-centimetre arm bends when light hits the algae embedded in it — no motor, no wires — and has grasped and lifted small objects.

What it is

TU Delft researchers built a soft robot arm about two centimetres long from two hydrogel layers, one embedded with living microalgae: when light hits, photosynthesis changes conditions inside the material, the layers swell unevenly, and the arm bends — no motor, no wires.

Evidence — Prototype

In demonstrations it grasped and lifted a rubber ring and a small metal ball, and it responds to changes in light direction and intensity. Paper in Nature Communications, reported by Knowridge, Oct 24–25, 2026.
