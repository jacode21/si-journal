Prediction Markets Have Traded More Than $1.4 Billion on the Midterms, One Day Before the Vote

Trading on 2026 midterm markets passed $1.4 billion in CNN's analysis — nearly $1.1 billion of it on Kalshi and more than $340 million on Polymarket. Tuesday's vote is the first full test of the platforms' claim to forecast elections, and of the regulators who have pledged to police insider trading on them.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 015  ·  Monday, November 2, 2026

More than $1.4 billion has been traded on prediction markets about the 2026 midterm elections, according to a CNN analysis of the platforms' public data — nearly $1.1 billion of it on Kalshi, the largest United States operator, and more than $340 million on Polymarket across its United States and offshore platforms. ForecastEx, owned by Interactive Brokers, accounts for more than $6.4 million. The figures measure trading volume — the value of contracts changing hands before markets close — not the sum originally wagered.

The structure of the trading matters more than the total. A nonprofit tracker, the Anti-Corruption Data Collective, counted 21,811 individual congressional markets across the platforms by September 30, with only about 16% of them on who wins a race; the majority trade on margins of victory, and more than $20 million had traded on outcomes that can be known in advance, such as candidate withdrawals and endorsements. Kalshi has suspended at least six 2026 candidates for betting on their own races, CNN reported, and federal regulators have pledged to act on insider trading around the midterms.

Tuesday is the settlement date for the cycle's central claim. Kalshi's chief executive, Tarek Mansour, has described the midterms as a test of market-based forecasting, and Polymarket cites a 98% accuracy rate for its election markets one day before an election — a company figure, carried here as the company's claim. Edition 013 reported Kalshi's $1 billion Series G at a $40 billion valuation and its stated share of the prediction market; what Tuesday adds is a public score. If the markets call the close races well, they enter the next cycle as a forecasting source news organizations cite alongside polls. If they miss, the $1.4 billion reads differently — as volume in search of a result.
