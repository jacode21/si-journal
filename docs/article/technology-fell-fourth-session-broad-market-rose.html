Technology Fell for a Fourth Straight Session While the Rest of the Market Rose Around It

On Thursday the S&P 500 fell 1.12% and the Nasdaq fell 1.94% — technology's fourth straight decline — while the Dow rose 0.77% and the Russell 2000 rose 1.46%. The VIX closed at 19.87, its highest since October 16; the 10-year Treasury yield stood at 4.90%.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 019  ·  Friday, November 6, 2026

Thursday's session drew the line this week has been sketching. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.94% to 24,676.06 and the S&P 500 fell 1.12% to 7,456.99, a fourth straight decline for both. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.77% to 52,383.36 and the Russell 2000, the small-company index, rose 1.46%. The VIX, the market's fear gauge, closed at 19.87, its highest close since October 16. The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.90%, and West Texas Intermediate crude fell about 4.3% to $91.97. This is not a selloff pattern, in which everything falls together. It is a sorting pattern: money leaving one trade and entering others on the same afternoon.

The trade being left is the one this paper has followed all autumn. Across the last two weeks of earnings, the suppliers of the AI buildout have been paid at record rates while the buyers absorbed the cost — and on Wednesday the broad market rallied on election clarity while several buildout names fell on their own reports. Thursday extended the point: the argument is no longer about whether AI demand exists. The reports settled that. It is about what serving the demand costs, who finances it, and at what interest rate — with the 10-year sitting just under 5% while it is decided.

Three dated tests now sit in a row. The October jobs report arrives Friday at 8:30 a.m., with economists surveyed expecting about 105,000 jobs added and unemployment of 4.7% — the last major print before the Federal Reserve's December meeting. Anthropic's roadshow follows in the week of November 9, setting the first public price for a frontier lab. Nvidia reports on November 17. If the sorting continues through those three, it stops looking like a rotation and starts looking like a repricing.
