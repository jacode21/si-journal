Amazon Signed a Deal for Decart's Chip Software and Its Founders, at a Reported Price of About $7.5 Billion

Amazon said on Monday, November 23, that it has agreed to acquire the technology behind Decart's Optimization Stack — software that helps AI models run faster and more cheaply on different makers' chips — and to bring Decart's three founders and its researchers and engineers into Amazon. Amazon did not disclose a price. Bloomberg, citing a person familiar with the matter, put the deal at about $7.5 billion. Amazon is not buying Decart's video and world models. The agreement still needs regulatory clearance. This paper reported the talks, at the same reported price, in Edition 036.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 037  ·  Tuesday, November 24, 2026

Decart was founded in 2023 in Israel by Dean Leitersdorf, Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev. Its public fame came from its models — Lucy, which edits live video, and Oasis, which generates a playable world in real time. The asset Amazon is buying is less visible. The Decart Optimization Stack, called DOS, sits between a model and the chip it runs on. It tunes the small programs, compilers and runtimes that carry out the model's work so that more of the chip's capacity is used instead of sitting idle between steps.

Amazon's reason is in its own statement. Decart has worked with Amazon since 2025 on Trainium, Amazon's own AI chip, and Amazon is preparing to sell Trainium chips to outside customers, not only use them in its own data centres. A chip is easier to sell when the software that runs models on it is good. Amazon said the technology will help customers train and run models faster and at lower cost on its custom silicon. The work will sit with Annapurna Labs, Amazon's chip unit, according to Globes.

Three terms need keeping separate. The price is reported, not disclosed: Bloomberg's figure is about $7.5 billion, Globes has reported above that, and neither company has confirmed any number. The purchase is of assets and people, not of the whole company: the models stay outside it, and what happens to them, and to Decart customers such as eBay, has not been said. And the founders' reported five-year stay, with part of the payment deferred across that period (Calcalist, November 18), means the price is partly a retention contract. The deal closes only after regulatory clearance, for which no date has been announced.

The Bigger Picture

One number from Edition 036 has changed its meaning. On Monday the $7.5 billion was the reported price of a possible purchase. Today it is the reported price of a signed one — and what it buys is narrower than the headline suggests: a software layer and a team of about a hundred people, not the models that made the company known. Amazon is paying for the part of Decart that makes other people's chips, including its own, easier to choose.
