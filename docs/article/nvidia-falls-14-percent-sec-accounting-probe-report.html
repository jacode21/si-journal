Nvidia Fell 14% to $125.53 After Reuters Reported an SEC Accounting Probe, Erasing About $491 Billion in One Day

Nvidia shares fell 14.01% on Friday, November 20, to close at $125.53, after Reuters reported that Securities and Exchange Commission enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at the company, citing two people briefed on the matter. The fall erased about $491 billion of market value in one session. It was Nvidia’s seventh straight daily fall and closed its worst week since 2008, down about 23%. The stock is now about 48% below its record close of $239.24 on October 6.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 034  ·  Saturday, November 21, 2026

The report landed in the afternoon of a week that was already going badly. Reuters reported on Friday, November 20, that the SEC’s enforcement staff are scrutinising potential accounting issues at Nvidia, citing two people briefed on the matter who spoke on condition of anonymity (Reuters, Nov 20). The Wall Street Journal had reported on Tuesday that the SEC was investigating the company, but could not say what the inquiry covered. Reuters said it could not determine when the inquiry opened or which accounting issues are involved. An SEC spokesperson declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately respond to Reuters. An investigation is not a finding of wrongdoing, and Reuters noted that many such inquiries close without enforcement action.

The market did not wait for an answer. Nvidia fell 14.01% to $125.53, its largest one-day fall since January 2025 and its lowest close since May 2025 (LSEG data via Reuters; Motley Fool market coverage, Nov 20). About $491 billion of market value was erased in the session, taking the company from about $3.52 trillion to about $3.03 trillion, and below Alphabet in value. The shares had been down more than 6% before the Reuters report published, and extended their losses after it. The week’s fall was about 23%, the stock’s worst week since 2008, and the shares are down about 48% from the October 6 record close — roughly $2.7 trillion of value gone in six weeks.

The probe is the third government action around the company in two days. On Thursday, California Attorney General Rob Bonta served Nvidia an investigative subpoena centred on whether the company or its former chief executive misled investors about advanced chips reaching China (Edition 033). The company’s quarterly report, the 10-Q, is still outstanding after its November 17 results, with a December 4 deadline. Former chief executive Jensen Huang, who resigned on November 12, is expected to face questioning by House committees on December 1. Open questions reported around the accounting file include how Nvidia reports revenue by geography and receivables running at about 64 days of sales; neither Reuters nor the Journal established that either is the probe’s subject, and this paper does not present them as its subject.

The fall was not Nvidia’s alone. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39, its seventh straight decline, and closed in correction territory, 11.6% below its October 6 record (Associated Press; Reuters preliminary closes). The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to about 22,618, turning negative for 2026, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.93% to 48,147.33. Broadcom, AMD and Micron fell between 12% and 14%. Anthropic, in its second day of trading, fell 8.5% (see the Latest item below). For the week, the S&P 500 lost 8.6%, the Nasdaq about 10% and the Dow 8% — the worst week for stocks since April 2025 on most counts, and since March 2020 for the Dow and Nasdaq on Yahoo Finance closes.

The Bigger Picture

Nvidia reported record revenue of $115.8 billion on Tuesday and guided the next quarter to $130 billion. None of that was in dispute on Friday. What the market repriced was not the quarter but the reliability of the reporting around the quarter — and in a market where one company’s revenue is the input to dozens of other companies’ spending plans, a question about one set of books travels further than a question about one company.
