Oracle Will Host Google’s AI Chips on Its Own Cloud Starting in 2027

Google’s eighth-generation TPUs will run on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure — their first home outside Google — while ChatGPT and Codex move into Oracle’s business applications. Oracle rose nearly 5% on the announcement.
Edited by Jordan Hale · Capital Editor  ·  Issue No. 010  ·  Wednesday, October 28, 2026

Oracle announced at its AI World conference in Las Vegas on Tuesday that Google’s Tensor Processing Units will be available through Oracle Cloud Infrastructure from calendar 2027, ramping across a multi-year deployment. It is the first time Google’s custom silicon will run on a competing hyperscale cloud. The deployment covers Google’s eighth-generation systems — the TPU 8t for training and the TPU 8i for inference — with the companies stating that the 8t offers nearly three times the compute performance per pod over the prior generation. Oracle will supply the data-center networking, power and cooling to host the accelerators at its sites.

The same announcement moved OpenAI’s products into Oracle’s software. ChatGPT and Codex are being integrated into Oracle’s enterprise applications, and OpenAI’s models are sold through Oracle’s Marketplace, payable with the prepaid Universal Credits its customers already hold. “More businesses are choosing OpenAI as we make it easier to put AI to work through the tools and systems they already use,” OpenAI president Greg Brockman said. CNBC reported that Oracle will also add OpenAI’s Jalapeño chip and Cerebras wafer-scale hardware to the OCI lineup, with Cerebras expected in the first half of 2027; those two additions rest on CNBC’s reporting rather than the companies’ joint announcement.

Oracle’s shares rose nearly 5% on Tuesday and Cerebras about 8%, according to market wraps of the announcement. The distinction the announcement itself draws is worth keeping: the TPU systems arrive in 2027. What Oracle has sold this week is a position — its cloud as the place where a customer can rent Nvidia, Google, Cerebras and OpenAI silicon side by side — a year or more before the hardware lands.
