AMD Reports Record Third-Quarter Revenue of $13.4 Billion as Data Center Sales Rise 97%

AMD's third quarter set company records: revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45%, and Data Center revenue of $8.5 billion, up 97%, both ahead of expectations. The fourth-quarter guide — about $17 billion — was the larger beat, and shares rose about 8% after hours.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 017  ·  Wednesday, November 4, 2026

AMD reported third-quarter results after Tuesday's close: revenue of $13.377 billion, up 45% from a year earlier and a company record, against about $13.1 billion expected — the consensus figure this paper carried into the report. Data Center revenue reached a record $8.546 billion, up 97% from a year earlier and 64% of the total, against about $8.19 billion expected, driven by EPYC server processors and the launch of the Instinct MI450 series of AI accelerators. GAAP net income was $2.43 billion, or $1.46 a share; on the company's adjusted basis, earnings were $2.09 a share against $1.95 expected. GAAP gross margin was 54%, and adjusted gross margin 56%.

The guide, not the quarter, is the news

For the fourth quarter AMD expects revenue of approximately $17 billion, plus or minus 3% — growth of about 66% from a year earlier and 27% from the third quarter, at an adjusted gross margin of about 55%. The guide sits well above the roughly $16.2 billion analysts had expected. Shares rose about 8% in after-hours trading. The response separates AMD's report from the two that preceded it this week: Apple and Palantir both beat on Monday and fell on their forward lines — a margin guide in one case, slower new-contract growth in the other. AMD's forward line was the beat.

What the quarter did not show

Two lines in the same release ran the other way. Client and Gaming revenue fell 8% to $3.722 billion, and gaming revenue alone fell 40% to $773 million, as memory costs weigh on consumer machines — the same pressure Apple cited in its December-quarter margin guide on Monday. Free cash flow fell to $554 million from $1.53 billion a year earlier, about 4% of revenue, in a quarter of record sales: growth at this speed consumes cash even at the supplier end of the buildout. Both figures are the company's, from its release and the coverage of it.

What the report establishes is demand at the supplier layer, stated in orders rather than in spending plans. The hyperscalers' results last week established what the buyers intend to spend; AMD's quarter is a receipt for part of that spending arriving, with a larger receipt promised for the fourth quarter. Whether the $17 billion guide is met is now a dated test — it will be answered with the fourth-quarter report, and Nvidia's report on November 17 is the next reading on the same demand.

The Bigger Picture

Read together, Monday and Tuesday drew the buildout's income statement from both sides. The buyers of compute were marked down on the cost of serving demand; the sellers of compute and networking were marked up on the revenue that cost creates. The argument has not changed — it is still about what growth costs — but for one day the cost showed up as someone else's record quarter.
