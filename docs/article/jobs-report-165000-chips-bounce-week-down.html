A Strong Jobs Report and a Chip Rebound Closed the Market's Worst Week Since June, Without Settling It

On Friday the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.09% to 24,579.42 and the S&P 500 rose 0.31% to 7,376.86, while the Dow fell 0.46% to 51,285.73. October payrolls rose 165,000 against a consensus near 65,000, unemployment was 4.1%, and the market's priced odds of a December rate increase rose to about 78%. For the week the S&P 500 fell 2.7% and the Nasdaq fell 3.4%.
Edited by Avery Lane · Frontier Editor  ·  Issue No. 020  ·  Saturday, November 7, 2026

Friday gave the week's argument its last two exhibits, and they point in opposite directions. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that employers added 165,000 jobs in October — against a Reuters consensus of about 65,000 — and that unemployment stood at 4.1%. August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000 jobs; September alone moved from 29,000 to 60,000. Professional and business services added 70,000 jobs, health care 42,000, and transportation and warehousing 25,000. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% in the month and 3.1% over the year. Nothing in the report resembles the softening labor market that would argue for lower interest rates.

The market's answer was split, in the pattern this paper has recorded all week. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.09% to 24,579.42 and the S&P 500 rose 0.31% to 7,376.86, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.46% to 51,285.73. The semiconductor index rose 4.2% — recovering about a third of Thursday's fall of more than 12% — with AMD up 11.04%, Lam Research up 8.23% after its board authorized a $25 billion buyback, Intel up 7.71% and Nvidia up 2.8%. Breadth ran the other way: 297 of the S&P 500's stocks fell against 202 that rose, and the equal-weighted index declined while the headline index gained. For the week, the S&P 500 fell 2.7% — its worst week since June — the Nasdaq fell 3.4% and the Dow fell 0.9%.

The rates market read the jobs report plainly. The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.86%, and the priced probability of a Federal Reserve rate increase at the December 8–9 meeting rose to about 78%, from roughly 67 to 70% before the release, according to Reuters. One part of Friday's rebound had a stated political source: buyers returned to AI infrastructure shares after National Economic Council director Kevin Hassett said the Democratic midterm wins will not stop the data-center buildout. The week's remaining question is therefore not about demand for labor or for chips — both printed strong. It is about the price of money while the buildout is financed, and the next readings are dated: the October consumer price index on Tuesday, November 10, Anthropic's roadshow in the week of November 9, and Nvidia's results on November 17.
