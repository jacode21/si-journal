The Position

The paper’s argument

The Demand Is Proven. The Financing Is the Open Question.

Three hyperscalers have now reported the same quarter, and the demand question is settled: Azure revenue grew 48%, Google Cloud revenue grew 103% and AWS revenue grew 39%, each at or above what analysts expected. What separates the reports is cash. Microsoft's free cash flow stayed positive at $18.6 billion. Alphabet's was negative $9.1 billion in the quarter. Amazon's was negative $42.4 billion over 12 months, in a quarter when it spent $77.3 billion and its operating income rose 61%. The returns are arriving as operating income while the costs leave as cash, and debt and equity markets are carrying the difference. The next test is today's OpenAI bridge signatures, where the financed layer prices the same gap.

The Toolkit

Useful tools for the next generation of professionals — consumer and enterprise, tested by what they change about the workday, not by their launch posts.

GitHub Copilot Fusion: The Model Picker That Chooses for You

GitHub made Copilot Fusion generally available this week, inside Visual Studio Code, the Copilot CLI, the Copilot app and JetBrains IDEs. Fusion is not a model; it is a router. A developer picks an effort level — Standard for everyday work, High for larger defined tasks, Max for the deepest reasoning — and Fusion assigns the job across the models Copilot carries, using one model for simple tasks, starting cheap and escalating to a stronger model when a judge model finds the draft wanting, or sending drafts to several models for critique before revision. Billing follows the models actually used, with no separate Fusion fee, and paid users get a 30% discount on Fusion model costs through November 30.

GitHub reports that Fusion Max scored within two points of Claude Opus 5.5 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at about half the cost and about 40% less time per task. That figure is the company's, from its launch materials; this paper has not run Fusion on its own work, and no independent run accompanied the launch. The workflow change is real either way: model selection — a decision developers have been making by hand, per task, for two years — moves inside the tool. The cost of that convenience is visibility: when a router writes the code, the reviewer has to ask which model wrote it before asking whether it is right. Fusion also lands in a busier Copilot: GitHub says agents opened more than 40% of pull requests in September, on 7.4 billion commits in the month.

One tool earns its place each day. Novelty doesn’t.

        About The Tomorrow Post

        The Tomorrow Post is a daily newspaper about the frontier — the models, markets and machines deciding what happens next. Its tagline is its standard: a newspaper for the digital age.

        What This Paper Is For

        Signal over noise

        Most days, a handful of developments actually matter. The paper's job is to find them, verify them, and say plainly why they matter — and to let the rest go. A smaller edition of true things beats a large one of almosts.

        Three readers at once

        Every story is written to serve an investor weighing what something is worth, a professional deciding what to use, and a curious reader who wants to understand. Money, use, wonder — if a story serves none of the three, it isn't signal.

        A record, not a feed

        Stories here are followed, not dropped. Developing stories keep their history in the open — assessments dated, revised, and answered for — and the paper builds knowledge from its own reporting, so each edition knows what the last one said.

        Measured by design

        No clickbait, no pop-ups, no engagement bait. The paper is built to be forwarded: it succeeds when a reader sends a story to a smart friend.

        How We Work, Briefly

        Every claim is sourced, and sources sit on the story page. Company claims are labeled as claims, figures carry their caveats, and unverified reports are marked as such. The paper is produced with AI tools and written under house bylines; the standards above apply to every line regardless. Corrections and tips: hello@thetomorrowpost.com.
